# The story of the Shattered Reef

The spine every quest, key, foe, island and line of dialogue hangs off. Tom's brief (2026-10-26): depth, size and
story beyond RuneScape; more Locke & Key and One Piece; made as if by a team of human writers and directors.

RuneScape is the bar for **scale** (a world you live in for years, quests with real stakes, a lore you piece together
yourself). Locke & Key is the bar for **wonder and dread** (keys that do impossible things, a house full of doors, a
voice behind one of them that wants out). One Piece is the bar for **adventure and heart** (a crew, a sea of strange
islands, rivals with dreams, a treasure everyone's chasing, bounties that say who you've become).

## The myth (what's true, though nobody knows all of it)

**The Varrow.** A thousand years ago the Shattered Reef was one land, the Varrow: a kingdom of harbours and bells.

**The Hollow Star.** It fell into the sea off the Varrow and split in two. One half sank to the bottom of the world. The other
half sang. Everything that heard the song (people, crabs, trees, stones) grew a **keyhole** to keep it out. That is why
every living thing in the Reef has one. A keyhole isn't a weakness. It's a door the song couldn't open.

**The Wardwrights.** Smiths of the Varrow found that iron from the star's fall could be cut into keys that open those
keyholes. Every key holds a **turn**, a single impossible thing it can do. They swore to cut keys only to heal, to
build and to defend. They kept that oath for three hundred years.

**The Choir.** The half of the star at the bottom of the sea isn't dead. It is many voices singing one song, and the
song wants in. Every keyhole is a door it is listening at. The Wardwrights built **the Bottom Door** over it, and ringed
the Varrow with **thirteen Locks** to keep the Door shut. Each Lock has one **Lockkey**.

**Corran Keel, the Black-Keel.** The greatest captain who ever lived, and the closest thing the sea ever had to a
king. He believed the Wardwrights had hidden the Varrow's treasury behind the Bottom Door (**Keel's Hoard**: "every
key ever cut, and the one that opens the sky"). He stole the thirteen Lockkeys one by one, with a crew that would
have followed him into the Door itself. At the twelfth Lock the Varrow broke apart. That was **the Shattering**. The
land became the Reef's islands; the Locks fell into the sea between them. Keel was never seen again. His ship,
the *Undertow*, was. Some say she still sails, crewed by men who don't age.

## What the player finds out, and when

The player starts knowing none of this. They piece it together from quests, **Keel's Log** (his scattered log
pages), the Keyring's lore lines, and what people let slip. Nobody in the world knows the whole truth, and some of
them are lying.

## Factions

- **The Harbourmasters' Compact.** Wynn, Sefa, Mercy, Jory and every harbourmaster after them. They ferry anyone,
  carry every letter, and take no side. They're the Reef's oldest institution and they know more than they say.
  Founded the day after the Shattering, to keep the islands talking to each other.
- **The Wardwrights' heirs.** A handful of families still carry the trade: Iven Marr (cartographer, Bellwether) is
  one, and doesn't yet know it. Their charts and their smithing are the player's way in.
- **The Hushed.** People who listen at keyholes until the Choir answers. They want the Bottom Door open and they
  believe it's mercy. The Key Collector worked for them. They wear no colours. They never raise their voices.
- **The Tides.** The seven captains with bounties over a hundred thousand. Rivals, not villains; each wants Keel's
  Hoard for a reason the player can understand (see below).
- **The island folk.** Every island has its own people, saints and monsters, built from real folklore (Cornish
  knockers, Caribbean douen and soucouyant, Norse draugr and nøkk). Their problems are local and personal, and
  they're where the big story touches ordinary lives.

Each one gets a ship, a crew, a bounty poster, a signature fight at sea **and** on land, and a personal quest that
explains them. Their bounties rise as yours does. Beat one and your bounty jumps; befriend one and their crew will
fly your colours in a fight.

## Rules for everyone who writes for the Reef

1. **Specific, not grand.** "The bell rang at low water. There is no bell at the Broken Ring." Not "an ancient
   evil stirs". Nobody in the Reef says "ancient evil".
2. **People talk like people.** Short lines. Contractions. They want something from you, and they have lives you
   only see the edge of. No one explains the plot to you on purpose.
3. **Every key has a cost or a catch.** A key that only helps is a coin. The Lockkeys all take something.
4. **Wonder first, dread second.** A key that lets you walk through a keyhole is delightful. Then you hear
   something on the other side.
5. **Folklore is respected.** Douen, soucouyant, draugr and knockers come from real traditions; use the real
   rules (salt, backward feet, knocks), not a monster-of-the-week skin.
6. **Leave gaps.** The player should be able to argue about what really happened at the twelfth Lock.
7. **The sea is a character.** Weather, tides and shallows change what's possible. A good plan is a sailing plan.

## Glossary

- **Turn**: the power held in a key. "That key has a mean turn."
- **Listening**: pressing your ear to a keyhole. Children are told never to. The Hushed do it all day.
- **Song-touched**: a creature the Choir has got into (the elites: Barnacled, Frenzied, Volatile).
- **Lockkey**: one of the thirteen keys to the Wardwrights' Locks. Keel's Tooth is the first one found.
- **Bell-time**: low water, when the Locks are nearest the surface and the bells ring with no bells.
